Video 3 · Observe and try

Observe and practice · In class · About 5 min · Evidence for the unit project

Four points on my globe

Materials　The globe you made in Lesson 2, four small labels, a pen.

  1. Use the four steps of “latitude first, then longitude” to find the four points below on your globe, and stick on numbered labels.
  2. Check each point once with each of the three ways: the letters, the neighbors, and another map (your atlas or a map app).
  3. Write down which country or city you think it is near, and take a photo to keep.
Sample answer:① 55°N, 37°E Moscow (Russia)　② 15°N, 32°E Khartoum (Sudan)　③ 33°S, 71°W Santiago (Chile)　④ 37°S, 175°E Auckland (New Zealand). Being a few degrees off is fine; nearby counts as found. If you landed on another continent, go back and see which mistake it was.

The teacher only checks whether the points are in the right place, not how neat the handiwork is.

06

Think · Retell

About 4 min
1. Retell In class
Explain to someone who has not taken this lesson: given “6°S, 107°E”, how do you find it on a globe step by step? And how do you check it once you have found it?
Mention at least: latitude first, the letters, how to estimate between two lines, and one way of checking.
Key points:Read the latitude first: S means south of the equator, so count south from the equator; 6° is between the equator and 10°S, a little closer to 10°S. Then read the longitude: E means east of the prime meridian, so count east to 107°, between 100°E and 110°E, close to 110°E. Where the two lines cross is the point: Jakarta. To check: S and E put it in the southern and eastern half, so it should be among the islands of Southeast Asia; it is not far southeast of Singapore; find it again on a flat map and it lands in the same place.
06

Think · Evaluate

In or after class
2. Evaluate
A classmate says, “A phone finds any place with one tap, so learning to read the grid is useless.” Do you agree?
Think: can a phone be wrong? When do you have only the numbers?
Key points:Phones can be wrong too: the driver stops on the wrong side of the road, and records with no position end up on “Null Island”. With no signal or no battery, a phone cannot help. When you read a book or an old map, or someone tells you a position, you often have only the numbers. Only someone who can read the grid can check what the phone says, and carry a picture of the whole Earth in their mind.

09

After class

Observe and practice · After class · About 15 min

Quiz your family

  1. Choose three places: your school, a big city, and a place you would like to learn about. Look up their latitude and longitude.
  2. Give a family member only the latitude and longitude, and ask them to find each place on a globe or map.
  3. You are the teacher: help them check with the three ways (letters, neighbors, another tool), and note which step they find hardest.

⚠ Do not write down where your home is. For the school, look up only the school gate or the city center.

Which step did your family member find hardest? How did you help them notice?
